About Eid al-Adha

🐑 Eid al-Adha 🐑

Eid al-Adha, also called the "Festival of Sacrifice," is one of the two major festivals in Islam. It falls at the end of the Hajj pilgrimage to Mecca.

Why is it celebrated?

The festival commemorates the willingness of the Prophet Ibrahim to sacrifice his son as an act of obedience to God, who provided a ram to sacrifice instead. It honors devotion, faith, and submission to God.

How Eid al-Adha is Observed
  1. Eid prayers - Special congregational prayers are held in the morning
  2. Qurbani - An animal, often a goat, sheep, or cow, is sacrificed
  3. Sharing the meat - The meat is divided among family, friends, and those in need
How People Celebrate
  • Attending special Eid prayers at the mosque
  • Performing the Qurbani (ritual sacrifice)
  • Distributing meat to family, friends, and those less fortunate
  • Wearing new or best clothes
  • Visiting family and exchanging greetings
A Festival of Sacrifice and Sharing

At its heart, Eid al-Adha is about faith, obedience, and generosity, especially toward those in need.

Eid Mubarak! 🐑
