About Dussehra

🏹 Dussehra 🏹

Dussehra, also known as Vijayadashami, is a major Hindu festival celebrated on the tenth day of the Hindu month of Ashwin. It marks the end of the nine-night Navaratri festival.

Why is it celebrated?

Dussehra celebrates the victory of good over evil. In North India, it marks Lord Rama's victory over the demon king Ravana, while in other regions it celebrates Goddess Durga's defeat of the buffalo demon Mahishasura.

How Dussehra is Marked
  • Ramlila - Dramatic re-enactments of the life of Lord Rama performed in the days before
  • Effigy burning - Giant effigies of Ravana are burned in the evening
  • Durga idol immersion - In Bengal, Goddess Durga's idols are immersed in rivers
How People Celebrate
  • Watching or performing Ramlila plays
  • Burning effigies of Ravana, Kumbhakarna, and Meghnad
  • Visiting temples for prayers
  • Exchanging greetings and sweets with family
  • Starting new ventures, considered an auspicious day
The Triumph of Good

At its heart, Dussehra is a reminder that righteousness ultimately prevails over wrongdoing, no matter how long the struggle takes.

Happy Dussehra! 🏹
