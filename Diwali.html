About Diwali

🪔 Diwali - The Festival of Lights 🪔

Diwali, also called Deepavali, is one of the most popular festivals celebrated in India and by Indian communities around the world. The word "Diwali" means "a row of lights."

Why is it celebrated?

Diwali celebrates the return of Lord Rama to his kingdom after 14 years of exile, and his victory over the demon king Ravana. People lit oil lamps, called "diyas," to welcome him home. That is why lighting lamps is still the main tradition today.

The 5 Days of Diwali
  1. Dhanteras - Cleaning the house and buying gold or new items
  2. Naraka Chaturdashi - Also called "Chhoti Diwali" (Small Diwali)
  3. Lakshmi Puja - The main day, with prayers, lights, and fireworks
  4. Govardhan Puja - Sometimes celebrated as the new year
  5. Bhai Dooj - A day for brothers and sisters
How People Celebrate
  • Lighting diyas (oil lamps) around the house
  • Making rangoli (colorful patterns) at the doorstep
  • Wearing new clothes
  • Sharing sweets like ladoo and barfi with family and neighbors
  • Bursting fireworks at night
  • Praying to Goddess Lakshmi for wealth and good fortune
A Festival of Light Over Darkness

At its heart, Diwali is about good winning over evil, and light winning over darkness. That is why homes are filled with lamps, candles, and fairy lights every year during this festival.

Happy Diwali! ✨
