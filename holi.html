About Holi

🎨 Holi 🎨

Holi, also called the "Festival of Colors," is a vibrant Hindu spring festival celebrated mainly in India and Nepal. It is known for people throwing colored powder and water at one another in joyful celebration.

Why is it celebrated?

Holi celebrates the arrival of spring, the triumph of good over evil, and the eternal love of the Hindu deities Radha and Krishna. The night before Holi, a bonfire called Holika Dahan is lit to symbolize the burning away of evil.

How Holi Unfolds
  1. Holika Dahan - A bonfire is lit the evening before, symbolizing the defeat of evil
  2. Rangwali Holi - The main day, filled with colored powders, water balloons, and music
  3. Sharing sweets - Families and friends exchange traditional treats like gujiya
How People Celebrate
  • Throwing colored powders (gulal) at friends and family
  • Spraying colored water using water guns (pichkaris)
  • Dancing to music in the streets
  • Lighting the Holika bonfire the night before
  • Eating festive sweets and snacks
A Festival of Joy and Renewal

At its heart, Holi is about forgetting old grudges, forgiving others, and welcoming the freshness of spring with color and laughter.

Happy Holi! 🎨
