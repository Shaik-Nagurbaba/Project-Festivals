About Ramzan

🌙 Ramzan 🌙

Ramzan, known in Arabic as Ramadan, is the ninth month of the Islamic lunar calendar and is considered the holiest month for Muslims around the world. It is a time of fasting, prayer, and reflection.

Why is it celebrated?

Muslims observe Ramzan because it is the month in which the Quran, the holy book of Islam, is believed to have first been revealed to the Prophet Muhammad. Fasting during this month is one of the Five Pillars of Islam.

Key Moments of Ramzan
  1. Suhoor - A meal eaten before dawn, just before the fast begins
  2. Roza (the fast) - No food or water from sunrise to sunset
  3. Iftar - The meal that breaks the fast each evening, often shared with family
  4. Laylat al-Qadr - The "Night of Power," believed to be the night the Quran was first revealed
  5. Eid al-Fitr - The festival marking the end of Ramzan, celebrated with prayers and feasting
How People Celebrate
  • Fasting from dawn to sunset
  • Extra night prayers called Taraweeh
  • Reading the Quran
  • Giving to charity (Zakat and Sadaqah)
  • Breaking the fast together as a family or community
  • Celebrating Eid al-Fitr with new clothes and sweets
A Month of Discipline and Gratitude

At its heart, Ramzan is about self-discipline, patience, and gratitude. Fasting is meant to build empathy for those who are less fortunate and to strengthen one's connection to faith.

Ramadan Mubarak! 🌙
