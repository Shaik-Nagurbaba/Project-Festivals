About Durga Puja

🙏 Durga Puja 🙏

Durga Puja is one of the most important festivals in Bengal, India, and among Bengali communities worldwide, celebrating Goddess Durga over several days of elaborate worship and festivity.

Why is it celebrated?

Durga Puja celebrates the goddess Durga's victory over the buffalo demon Mahishasura, symbolizing the triumph of good over evil. It falls during the same period as Navaratri and leads up to Dussehra.

Key Days of Durga Puja
  1. Shashthi - The goddess's idol is unveiled in elaborately decorated pandals
  2. Saptami, Ashtami, Navami - Days of prayer, cultural events, and community gatherings
  3. Vijaya Dashami - The idol is taken in procession and immersed in a river or water body
How People Celebrate
  • Visiting elaborately themed pandals to see the Durga idol
  • Attending cultural programs, music, and dance performances
  • Wearing new clothes and visiting family
  • Taking part in the idol immersion procession (visarjan)
  • Sharing festive meals and sweets
A Celebration of Strength

At its heart, Durga Puja celebrates courage, strength, and the victory of righteousness, brought to life through art, devotion, and community.
