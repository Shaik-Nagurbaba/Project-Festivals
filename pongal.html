About Pongal

🌾 Pongal 🌾

Pongal is a four-day harvest festival celebrated mainly in Tamil Nadu, India, marking the end of the harvest season. It is named after the traditional dish made by boiling rice with milk and jaggery.

Why is it celebrated?

Pongal is celebrated to thank the Sun God and nature for a good harvest. It marks the beginning of the sun's six-month-long northward journey, known as Uttarayana.

The 4 Days of Pongal
  1. Bhogi - Old belongings are discarded and bonfires are lit to welcome new beginnings
  2. Thai Pongal - The main day, when the Pongal dish is cooked and offered to the Sun God
  3. Mattu Pongal - A day dedicated to honoring and decorating cattle
  4. Kaanum Pongal - A day for family outings and visiting relatives
How People Celebrate
  • Cooking the traditional Pongal dish in a clay pot until it overflows
  • Decorating homes with kolam (rice flour patterns) at the doorstep
  • Decorating and honoring cattle
  • Bonfires and community gatherings
  • Visiting family and exchanging greetings
Gratitude for the Harvest

At its heart, Pongal is a festival of thanksgiving, celebrating nature, the sun, and the hard work of farmers.
