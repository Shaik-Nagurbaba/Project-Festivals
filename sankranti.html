About Makar Sankranti

🪁 Makar Sankranti 🪁

Makar Sankranti is a harvest festival celebrated across India, marking the sun's transition into the zodiac sign of Capricorn (Makar). It is one of the few Hindu festivals that falls on a fixed date, usually January 14th.

Why is it celebrated?

The festival celebrates the sun's movement toward the Northern Hemisphere, which is seen as the start of longer, warmer days. It is celebrated under different names across India, including Pongal, Lohri, and Bihu.

How It Is Celebrated Across India
  • Kite flying - Skies fill with colorful kites, especially in Gujarat and Rajasthan
  • Til-gud sweets - Sesame and jaggery sweets are made and shared
  • Holy dips - Many take a ritual bath in rivers like the Ganges
How People Celebrate
  • Flying kites in competitions with family and friends
  • Making and sharing til-gud (sesame-jaggery) sweets
  • Taking a holy dip in a river
  • Bonfires and folk songs in some regions
  • Visiting temples for prayers
Welcoming Longer Days

At its heart, Makar Sankranti is about renewal, gratitude for the harvest, and welcoming the warmth of longer days ahead.

Happy Makar Sankranti! 🪁
