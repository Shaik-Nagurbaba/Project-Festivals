About Navaratri

💃 Navaratri 💃

Navaratri, meaning "nine nights," is a Hindu festival dedicated to the worship of Goddess Durga in her nine different forms. It is celebrated differently across various regions of India.

Why is it celebrated?

Navaratri celebrates the victory of Goddess Durga over the buffalo demon Mahishasura, symbolizing the triumph of good over evil. The festival culminates in Dussehra on the tenth day.

The Nine Nights
  • Garba and Dandiya Raas - Traditional dances performed in Gujarat every evening
  • Durga Puja - In Bengal, elaborately decorated pandals house idols of Durga
  • Golu - In South India, dolls and figurines are displayed on tiered steps
How People Celebrate
  • Dancing garba and dandiya raas at night
  • Fasting for all or some of the nine days
  • Worshipping the nine forms of Goddess Durga
  • Displaying doll arrangements (golu) in South India
  • Visiting decorated pandals in Bengal
Celebrating Divine Feminine Power

At its heart, Navaratri honors strength, courage, and the victory of good over evil through the worship of the divine feminine.
